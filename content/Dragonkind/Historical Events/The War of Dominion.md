# The War of Dominion

## Overview

The War of Dominion was the greatest conflict ever fought between Dragonkind and Heaven.

Though countless wars have scarred the Mortal Realm throughout history, none approached the scale, devastation, or consequences of the War of Dominion.

At its heart, the conflict was not a struggle for territory.

Nor was it a battle for wealth, power, or conquest.

It was a war over a single question:

> Who, if anyone, possesses the right to guide creation?

For Dragonkind, the answer was clear.

Creation belongs to itself.

For many within Heaven, the answer was far more complicated.

The resulting conflict would shape the relationship between Dragons and the Celestial Court for ages to come.

---

## Origins

The roots of the war can be traced to differing interpretations of stewardship.

Both Heaven and Dragonkind believed they served creation.

Both believed they sought its flourishing.

Yet their philosophies increasingly diverged.

Many Dragons viewed creation as something that should be protected but never controlled.

This belief formed the foundation of the [[Crucible of Creation]].

Within portions of Heaven, however, a different perspective emerged.

Some argued that creation required stronger guidance.

That the Mortal Realm could not be trusted to navigate its own future.

That intervention was not merely justified but necessary.

As these disagreements deepened, tensions between Dragonkind and Heaven grew increasingly difficult to ignore.

---

## The Question of Dominion

The conflict ultimately centered around the concept of dominion.

To many Dragons, dominion represented the belief that creation could be directed according to the will of those who considered themselves wiser than it.

This principle violated one of the most sacred teachings of the Crucible:

> Creation belongs to creation.

Many Dragons feared that even well-intentioned dominion would eventually become control.

Control would become ownership.

Ownership would become tyranny.

Heaven rejected this characterization.

Many within the Celestial Court argued that stewardship sometimes required intervention.

What followed was not merely a political disagreement.

It was a philosophical divide.

---
## Unification of Dragonkind

Though the War of Dominion is most often remembered for its impact on the relationship between Dragonkind and Heaven, many Dragons consider its greatest legacy to be the unification of Dragon civilization itself.

Prior to the War of Dominion, Dragonkind possessed no unified institution through which all Broods could collectively act.

The Elder Broods maintained relationships with one another and often cooperated when necessary, but each lineage remained largely responsible for its own affairs.

Most Dragons identified first with their Brood and only secondarily with Dragonkind as a whole. The pressures of the conflict demonstrated that the Broods could no longer act solely as independent lineages when matters affecting all of Dragonkind were at stake.

In response, the Elder Broods established the Conclave of Scales.

For the first time in history, the Regents and representatives of the Broods possessed a unified institution through which they could coordinate, deliberate, and speak with a collective voice.

For this reason, many historians regard the War of Dominion not only as a conflict with Heaven, but as the event that transformed the Elder Broods into a truly unified Dragon civilization.

The newly formed Conclave eventually established its seat within Wyrmreach, creating a permanent gathering place through which the Elder Broods could coordinate while preserving their individual identities.

---

## The Opening Conflict

When diplomacy finally failed, isolated confrontations escalated into open war.

The first battles shocked both sides.

Many within Heaven underestimated the strength of the Dragon Regents.

Many Dragons underestimated the resources available to Heaven.

The scale of the conflict rapidly exceeded expectations.

Entire celestial hosts were mobilized.

Ancient Dragons emerged from seclusion.

Regents took to the battlefield.

For the first time since the earliest ages of creation, powers capable of reshaping entire regions stood openly opposed to one another.

---

## The Dragon Regents

The Dragon Regents proved to be among the most formidable forces in the conflict.

Each Regent embodied a fundamental manifestation of creation itself.

Their power rivaled that of the greatest divine beings.

It soon became apparent that defeating a single Regent required extraordinary sacrifice.

In many engagements, entire hosts of Lesser Gods were required to challenge one Regent directly.

Elsewhere, multiple Gods were forced to cooperate simply to achieve parity against a single Regent.

These realities shocked many within Heaven and contributed significantly to the mounting costs of the war.

---

## The Cost of Victory

As the conflict continued, a troubling realization emerged.

Neither side could achieve victory without paying a catastrophic price.

Heaven possessed immense resources and divine influence.

Dragonkind possessed the Regents, the Broods, and an unwavering commitment to the Crucible.

Every battle consumed lives, knowledge, and ancient institutions.

Even apparent victories often came at unbearable cost.

The war gradually transformed from a question of who could win into a question of what would remain afterward.

---
## The Turning Point

As the war continued, leaders on both sides began to recognize a troubling reality.

Victory was becoming increasingly meaningless.

Every battle inflicted wounds upon creation itself.

Ancient knowledge vanished.

Entire regions were scarred.

Institutions that had endured for ages disappeared beneath the weight of the conflict.

For the first time, serious voices within both Heaven and Dragonkind began asking whether victory remained worth its cost.

Many historians regard this realization as the true beginning of the war's end.

---
## The Cradle Question

As the conflict escalated, many Dragons grew increasingly concerned about a possibility previously considered unthinkable.

What if the war eventually threatened the [[The Cradle of Scales]]?

The Cradle represented the future of Dragonkind itself.

No victory could justify risking its destruction.

This realization profoundly influenced draconic strategy during the latter years of the conflict.

Protecting the Cradle became a priority greater than victory itself.

Many historians believe this shift helped create the conditions necessary for peace.

---

## The End of the War

The War of Dominion did not end through conquest.

Neither Heaven nor Dragonkind emerged victorious.

Instead, the conflict ended when both sides recognized a difficult truth:

Creation would not survive an endless war between its greatest guardians.

Peace negotiations eventually brought the conflict to a close.

Compromises were made.

Boundaries were established.

Wounds remained.

Though open warfare ceased, the philosophical disagreements that caused the conflict were never fully resolved.

---

## Legacy

The War of Dominion remains one of the defining events in Dragon history.

Its lessons continue to shape the relationship between Heaven and Dragonkind.

For Dragons, the war serves as a reminder that even noble intentions can become dangerous when paired with excessive authority.

For Heaven, it serves as a reminder that wisdom alone does not grant ownership.

Even today, many debates within the Conclave of Scales eventually invoke the lessons of the war.

The conflict may be over.

Its questions are not.

---

## Relationship to the Crucible of Creation

The War of Dominion is often viewed as the ultimate test of the Crucible.

Dragonkind's refusal to abandon its principles came at tremendous cost.

Yet many Dragons believe the alternative would have cost far more.

The war reinforced one belief above all others:

Stewardship and dominion are not the same thing.

The distinction remains central to Dragon philosophy even now.

---

## Common Saying

> "Creation survives because neither side was willing to destroy it."
