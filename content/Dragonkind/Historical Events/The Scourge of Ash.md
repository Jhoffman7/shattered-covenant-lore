# The Scourge of Ash

## Overview

The Scourge of Ash was the Second Recorded Near-Cataclysm and remains one of the most extensively studied crises in Dragon history.

Unlike many disasters that threatened creation through destruction, the Scourge threatened creation through stagnation.

During the event, regions across the Mortal Realm gradually lost their ability to recover, renew, and transform.

What began as isolated anomalies eventually spread across multiple civilizations and ecosystems, creating a crisis that endangered far more than any single people or nation.

Though the catastrophe was ultimately contained, the Scourge of Ash remains one of the clearest examples of how even fundamental principles of creation can become destabilized.

---

## The Failure of Renewal

The defining characteristic of the Scourge of Ash was the gradual breakdown of renewal itself.

Throughout creation, endings are normally followed by beginnings.

Forests regrow after fires.

Communities rebuild after hardship.

Wounds heal.

Life adapts.

The Scourge disrupted this cycle.

Regions affected by the phenomenon became trapped in states of decline.

Damage accumulated.

Recovery ceased.

Transformation stalled.

The natural progression from ending to beginning no longer functioned as expected.

Though death remained possible, rebirth became increasingly rare.

---

## Early Signs

The first recorded manifestations appeared within isolated regions of the Mortal Realm.

Entire forests failed to regenerate after seasonal fires.

Farmland exhausted by harvests produced little new growth.

Communities devastated by hardship struggled to recover despite possessing the resources necessary to rebuild.

At first these incidents appeared unrelated.

Only over time did scholars and observers begin recognizing a disturbing pattern.

The affected regions were not merely suffering misfortune.

They were losing their capacity for renewal.

---

## Recognition by the Ashwings

Among the Elder Broods, the Ashwings were the first to recognize the true nature of the crisis.

To many observers, the Scourge appeared to be an ecological or societal disaster.

The Ashwings saw something far more alarming.

They recognized that the cycle connecting destruction, transformation, and renewal was beginning to fail.

For a Brood whose philosophy centered upon rebirth and new beginnings, the implications were immediately obvious.

If endings could no longer give rise to beginnings, creation itself would eventually stagnate.

Their warnings would ultimately prove correct.

---

## Escalation

As decades passed, the phenomenon spread.

Entire regions of the Mortal Realm became trapped in decline.

Ancient forests remained barren.

Cities failed to recover from disasters that would normally have been temporary setbacks.

Generations inherited the consequences of destruction without ever witnessing restoration.

More concerning still, isolated manifestations eventually appeared within portions of the Broodlands.

Though far less severe than those affecting the Mortal Realm, their presence demonstrated that the crisis was no longer confined to mortal civilizations.

For the first time, Dragonkind recognized the event as a threat to creation itself.

---

## The Response

The Ashwings were among the first to investigate the phenomenon, but they did not face it alone.

As evidence mounted, multiple Elder Broods contributed knowledge, resources, and expertise toward understanding the crisis.

Though the Conclave of Scales did not yet exist, the Scourge of Ash became one of the earliest examples of widespread cooperation between the Elder Broods in response to a shared existential threat.

Over many years, Dragons, scholars, and stewards worked to halt the spread of the phenomenon and restore stability to the affected regions.

The exact methods used remain restricted within Dragon records.

---

## Containment

Eventually the spread of the Scourge was halted.

Recovery returned to the affected regions.

The cycle of destruction and renewal resumed.

Though scars remained for generations, creation gradually healed.

The crisis was officially classified as the Second Recorded Near-Cataclysm.

Subsequent investigations concluded that, had the phenomenon continued unchecked, the consequences might have extended far beyond the Mortal Realm.

For this reason, much of the knowledge surrounding the event was sealed by later generations.

---

## Legacy

The Scourge of Ash left a lasting mark upon Dragon philosophy.

Many of the lessons associated with Ashwing teachings concerning transformation, resilience, and renewal trace their origins to this period.

The event reinforced a truth that Dragons continue to teach even in the modern age:

Destruction is not the enemy.

Stagnation is.

Creation can survive endings.

What it cannot survive is the loss of new beginnings.

---

## Common Saying

> "Ash is not feared because things end.
>
> Ash is feared because endings must be followed by beginnings."
