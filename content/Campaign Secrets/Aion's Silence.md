# Aion's Silence

**Status:** Secret Lore

The history of the Covenant contains many tragedies.

None are greater than the silence of Aion.

The Keeper of Ages.

The Witness of Time.

The one being most capable of knowing when the appointed hour had arrived.

The one being who chose not to speak.

---

## The Architect Of The Covenant

Among the First Twelve, it was Aion who proposed the Covenant.

He did not seek the imprisonment of Xyros.

He did not seek eternal stability.

He sought time.

A period during which civilization could mature.

A period during which history could establish roots.

A period during which reality could become strong enough to withstand the Turning.

The Covenant was intended as a temporary solution.

Nothing more.

---

## The Future He Saw

As ages passed, Aion continued to watch the flow of time.

He witnessed countless futures.

Some beautiful.

Some terrible.

Some impossible to comprehend.

One future returned again and again.

A future beyond the Covenant.

A future beyond certainty.

A future in which the influence of Heaven gradually diminished.

---

## The Age Beyond Heaven

In the futures Aion witnessed, the gods did not perish.

They were not overthrown.

They were not forgotten.

They simply became less central.

Mortals governed themselves.

Dragon Regents lost their divine mandates.

Saints arose without the approval of the Celestial Court.

New forms of magic emerged.

New philosophies appeared.

The future increasingly belonged to those who lived within it.

Rather than those who watched over it.

---

## Fear

Aion did not fear destruction.

He feared irrelevance.

Not for himself.

For Heaven.

For the institutions he had helped build.

For the civilizations he had watched grow across thousands of years.

For the order that had emerged from the Covenant.

He feared that ending the Covenant would unravel everything he loved.

---

## The First Time

Eventually the question was asked.

> "Has it been long enough?"

Aion looked toward the future.

And answered:

> "Not yet."

At the time, he believed it.

---

## The Second Time

The question came again.

The world had grown.

Civilization had flourished.

The roots had deepened.

Still Aion hesitated.

Still the future troubled him.

Again he answered:

> "Not yet."

---

## The Long Silence

Centuries became millennia.

The question continued.

The answer remained unchanged.

Over time, fewer beings remembered why the question mattered.

Fewer remembered the promise.

Fewer remembered the purpose.

Aion noticed.

Aion understood.

Aion said nothing.

---

## The Choice

No record exists of the exact moment Aion made his decision.

Perhaps no such moment exists.

Perhaps it happened gradually.

A century at a time.

A generation at a time.

A single unanswered question at a time.

The result remained the same.

The promise faded.

The Covenant endured.

And Aion allowed it.

---

## Umbros

The burden of Aion's silence is most visible in Umbros.

Again and again, Umbros placed his trust in the Keeper of Ages.

Again and again he asked:

> "Has it been long enough?"

Again and again Aion answered:

> "Not yet."

Because Umbros trusted him, the burden continued.

Because Umbros trusted him, the promise remained unfulfilled.

---

## Sol'Vara

The burden of Aion's silence is also visible in Sol'Vara.

As centuries passed, memories faded.

Questions lost context.

Fragments disappeared.

Yet Sol continued asking.

> "Has it been long enough?"

Aion always understood what she truly meant.

Aion always knew who she was asking about.

Aion never answered.

---

## Regret

Aion does not view himself as innocent.

Neither does he view himself as a villain.

He understands what he allowed.

He understands what was lost.

He understands the cost.

The burden of this knowledge has followed him longer than most civilizations have existed.

Yet even now, Aion is uncertain.

Even now, he does not know whether he was wrong.

---

## The Tragedy

The tragedy of Aion is not that he broke the promise.

The tragedy is that he genuinely believed he was protecting the world.

The tragedy is that he may still believe it.
