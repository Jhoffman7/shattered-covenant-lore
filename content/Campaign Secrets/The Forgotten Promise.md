# The Forgotten Promise

**Status:** Secret Lore

The Forgotten Promise refers to the gradual loss of the Covenant's original purpose.

No single event caused the promise to be forgotten.

No great conspiracy erased it.

No war destroyed it.

No villain orchestrated it.

The promise was lost the same way many truths are lost.

Slowly.

---

## The Original Understanding

When the Covenant was signed, all parties understood its purpose.

The burden was temporary.

The Covenant existed to grant reality stability while civilization matured.

When the appointed time arrived, the Covenant would end.

Xyros would be released.

Umbros would be freed from his burden.

Reality would continue its natural course.

This was not a secret.

It was the foundation upon which the agreement was built.

---

## The First Centuries

For generations, the promise remained widely understood.

The First Twelve remembered.

The Celestial Court remembered.

The earliest Dragon Regents remembered.

The possibility of the Covenant ending was accepted as inevitable.

Most simply believed that day remained far in the future.

---

## The Rise Of Stability

As centuries passed, the Covenant appeared successful.

Civilizations endured.

Kingdoms flourished.

Knowledge accumulated.

Trade expanded.

Faith spread.

For the first time, history began building upon itself rather than constantly replacing itself.

Many looked upon this prosperity and concluded that the Covenant had been justified.

---

## The First Delay

Eventually the question arose.

> "Has the world become strong enough?"

Some argued yes.

Others argued no.

Most chose caution.

The answer became:

> "Not yet."

At the time, few considered this controversial.

The delay was viewed as temporary.

A small extension.

Nothing more.

---

## The Growth Of Institutions

As millennia passed, entire systems emerged around the Covenant.

The Dragon Regents derived authority from it.

The Celestial Court derived legitimacy from it.

Religious orders built doctrines around it.

Kingdoms structured laws around it.

Generations were born into a world shaped by the Covenant.

Few could imagine a world without it.

---

## The Fear Of Change

Over time, the question itself began to change.

The debate was no longer:

> "Has the Covenant fulfilled its purpose?"

The debate became:

> "What happens if it ends?"

The distinction was subtle.

But important.

Fear slowly replaced purpose.

The future became something to manage rather than embrace.

---

## The Fading Of Memory

As ages passed, fewer beings remained who remembered the original signing.

The First Twelve endured.

Most others did not.

Stories became myths.

Myths became traditions.

Traditions became assumptions.

The promise was not erased.

It simply ceased to be discussed.

---

## Sol'Vara's Question

Among the First Twelve, one voice continued to ask.

Again and again.

Across ages and generations.

The same question.

> "Has it been long enough?"

Even as memories faded, Sol'Vara continued asking.

Often she could not explain why.

Often she no longer remembered the full reason.

Yet the question remained.

A wound without a scar.

A memory without a name.

Something within her refused to let go.

---

## Velora's Concern

Velora recognized the danger before most.

She saw paths converging.

Questions disappearing.

Possibilities narrowing.

The future was becoming constrained.

Not by force.

By certainty.

Unable to preserve the promise directly, Velora began leaving fragments behind.

Clues.

Symbols.

Dreams.

Records.

Paths.

Not enough to reveal the truth.

Only enough to ensure it could someday be found again.

---

## A Promise Unkept

By the current age, few understand what was lost.

The Covenant remains.

The burden remains.

The promise does not.

Most believe the Covenant was always intended to be permanent.

Most believe Umbros willingly accepted an eternal responsibility.

Most believe Xyros was meant to remain imprisoned forever.

None of these beliefs are true.

---

## The Greatest Failure

The tragedy of the Forgotten Promise is not that someone broke it.

The tragedy is that almost no one remembers it was ever made.

The burden continued.

The prison endured.

The world moved forward.

And somewhere beyond memory, a promise waited to be honored.
