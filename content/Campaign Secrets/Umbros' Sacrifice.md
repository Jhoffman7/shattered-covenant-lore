# Umbros' Sacrifice

**Status:** Secret Lore

The sacrifice of Umbros is considered one of the most important events in the history of creation.

Few beings remember it.

Fewer still understand it.

The modern Celestial Court teaches that the Covenant simply exists.

The truth is far more personal.

---

## The Burden Of The Covenant

When the First Twelve agreed to create the Covenant, a problem became immediately apparent.

The Covenant required an anchor.

The influence of Xyros could not simply be restrained through divine will alone.

The agreement required a living vessel capable of bearing the burden.

Someone would have to become the lock.

Someone would have to stand between reality and the Turning.

---

## The Silence

The First Twelve debated who should bear the burden.

No consensus could be reached.

The responsibility was immense.

The duration unknown.

Many feared what such a sacrifice would ultimately become.

For a time, no one volunteered.

---

## Umbros Steps Forward

The silence ended when Umbros offered himself.

Accounts differ on his exact words.

All surviving versions agree on one fact.

He volunteered willingly.

Umbros believed the Covenant served a necessary purpose.

He understood the need for stability.

He understood the value of sacrifice.

Most importantly, he trusted that the promise would be honored.

The burden was meant to be temporary.

Someone had to carry it.

Umbros chose to be that someone.

---

## Sol'Vara's Objection

Among the First Twelve, Sol'Vara opposed the decision most strongly.

Not because she disagreed with the Covenant.

Not because she doubted Umbros.

Because she feared the cost.

Ancient fragments suggest she repeatedly questioned the duration of the burden.

Many accounts describe long conversations between Sol'Vara and Umbros before the signing.

The contents of those conversations have been lost.

Only fragments remain.

One surviving fragment records Umbros speaking the following words:

> "I will return."

Another records Sol asking:

> "How long?"

To which Umbros reportedly answered:

> "Not long enough to matter."

---

## The Forging Of The Anchor

The Covenant could not contain the burden in its entirety.

To preserve both the Covenant and Umbros himself, portions of his divine essence were separated and transformed into sacred anchors.

These anchors became physical manifestations of what made Umbros who he was.

His memories.

His promises.

His hopes.

His identity.

His love.

His capacity for doubt.

These anchors were scattered and hidden.

Over time their true purpose was forgotten.

---

## Becoming The Lock

At the moment of the signing, Umbros ceased to be merely a Primordial.

His essence became interwoven with the Covenant itself.

The distinction between Umbros and the prison began to blur.

As centuries became millennia, portions of his mind, memories, and identity became fragmented.

The burden endured.

Umbros endured.

---

## Trust

The greatest tragedy of Umbros' sacrifice was not the burden itself.

It was trust.

Umbros believed the Covenant would end when its purpose had been fulfilled.

Whenever doubt arose, he turned to Aion.

The Keeper of Ages.

The witness to time.

The one best positioned to know when the appointed moment had arrived.

Again and again, Umbros asked the same question.

> "Has it been long enough?"

Again and again, the answer came.

> "Not yet."

And because he trusted Aion, Umbros continued to endure.

---

## The Forgotten God

As ages passed, history changed.

Records vanished.

Memories faded.

The original purpose of the Covenant became obscured.

The promise was forgotten.

The burden remained.

By the current age, even many among Heaven no longer understand the true nature of Umbros' sacrifice.

Some remember him as a Primordial.

Some remember him as a myth.

Few understand that the Covenant itself still bears his name.

---

## The Final Tragedy

Umbros never broke his promise.

He never abandoned the Covenant.

He never sought release.

He never demanded freedom.

He simply waited.

Believing the day would eventually come.

Believing the promise would eventually be honored.

Believing that someone would remember.
