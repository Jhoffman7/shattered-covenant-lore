# The First Twelve

The First Twelve are the oldest known divine beings in existence.

Known throughout history as the Firstborn Powers, the Eternal Court, and the Architects of Reality, they are believed to be the foundation upon which all existence rests.

Unlike the Celestial Gods, the First Twelve do not merely govern concepts.

They are those concepts.

Death exists because Nethrys exists.

Truth exists because Elyndra exists.

Time exists because Aion exists.

Even the [[The Celestial Court|Celestial Gods]], [[Dragon Regents]], and the greatest saints exist beneath their influence.

Whether the First Twelve created reality or emerged alongside it remains one of the oldest and most fiercely debated theological questions in the world.

---

## The Great Divide

Though united in their responsibility for reality, the First Twelve do not share a single vision for how existence should function.

Over countless ages, their philosophies have formed three broad ideological alignments:

### The Concordant

- [[Aion|Aion, Keeper of Ages]]
    
- [[Tharos|Tharos, the Golden Law]]
    
- [[Elyndra|Elyndra, Keeper of Truths]]
    

### The Ascendant

- [[Vaelor|Vaelor, the Open Hand]]
    
- [[Morwyn|Morwyn, Lady of Dreams]]
    
- [[Kael'Thur|Kael'Thur, Lord of Strife]]
    
- [[Thaloris|Thaloris, the Ever-Tide]]
    

### The Balance

- [[Sol'Vara|Sol'Vara, the First Light]]
    
- [[Umbros|Umbros, the Hollow King]]
    
- [[Velora|Velora, Weaver of Paths]]
    
- [[Ilyria|Ilyria, Heart of Life]]
    
- [[Nethrys|Nethrys, the Quiet End]]
    

Collectively, these divisions are known as [[The Great Divide]].

These alignments are not formal organizations, but rather schools of divine thought that continue to shape the politics of Heaven, the actions of the Celestial Court, and the beliefs of countless mortal civilizations.

The Great Divide concerns a single fundamental question:

> What should happen when a thing has fulfilled its purpose?

The answer to this question has shaped the history of reality itself.

The Concordant believes stability must be preserved.

The Ascendant believes change must eventually be embraced.

The Balance seeks wisdom between the two.

Though the Divide began during the earliest ages of creation, it remains unresolved even now.

Many believe its ultimate resolution will determine the future of the world.

## Sol'Vara, the First Light

**Domain:** Creation

**Titles:**

- The First Light
    
- Mother of Beginnings
    
- The Eternal Dawn
    
- Architect of Possibility
    
- The Star-Bearer
    

**Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

The eldest of the First Twelve.

Sol'Vara embodies creation, invention, inspiration, hope, and beginnings. She is often depicted as warm, compassionate, endlessly curious, and deeply invested in the growth of both gods and mortals.

Many religions portray Sol'Vara as the architect of reality and the mother of civilization itself.

Where others see limitations, Sol'Vara sees possibilities.

Her greatest strength is her ability to imagine what could be.

Her greatest weakness is her tendency to create before fully considering the consequences.

Ancient myths teach that before the world existed, there was only Sol'Vara and [[Umbros|Umbros, the Hollow King]].

Their relationship is considered one of the oldest and most important bonds in existence and remains a central topic within [[Divine Relationships]].

Though she supported the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]], Sol'Vara opposed the burden placed upon Umbros more strongly than any of the other Primordials.

As ages passed and memories faded, she continued asking a question she could no longer fully explain:

> "Has it been long enough?"

Few understand why she asks.

Even Sol'Vara herself no longer remembers the complete answer.

**Philosophy:**  
Every great thing begins with an act of creation.

---

## Umbros, the Hollow King

**Domain:** Void

**Titles:**

- The Hollow King
    
- Keeper of Silence
    
- Lord of the Unseen
    
- The Endless Shadow
    
- The Watcher Beyond the Stars
    

**Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

The second eldest of the First Twelve.

Umbros embodies mystery, absence, limitation, consequence, sacrifice, and the unknown.

Though feared by many mortals, philosophers and scholars often view him as one of the most necessary forces in existence.

Where Sol'Vara asks what can be created, Umbros asks what consequences will follow.

He is not cruel.

He is not evil.

He simply refuses to ignore uncomfortable truths.

When the First Twelve forged the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]], Umbros volunteered to become its anchor.

No one commanded him.

No one forced him.

He chose.

Believing the burden would be temporary, Umbros accepted a sacrifice that would eventually become the foundation of the Covenant itself.

Over time, portions of his divine essence were separated into the relics now known as [[The Anchors of Umbros]].

As millennia passed, Umbros became increasingly intertwined with the Covenant.

Much of his identity fragmented.

Many forgot him entirely.

Yet he continued to endure.

Waiting for the day the promise would finally be honored.

**Philosophy:**  
Every light casts a shadow.

---

## Aion, Keeper of Ages

**Domain:** Time

**Titles:**

- Keeper of Ages
    
- The Eternal Witness
    
- Lord of Hours
    
- The Unending Clock
    

**Alignment:** [[The Concordant]]

Aion embodies the passage of time, memory, history, and continuity.

He observes all things and forgets nothing.

Unlike many of the First Twelve, Aion rarely interferes directly in worldly affairs.

He sees all events as part of a greater continuum.

To Aion, empires rise and fall in the same way seasons change.

It was Aion who originally proposed the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]].

His intention was never to create an eternal prison.

He sought only to provide reality sufficient time to mature.

Over countless ages, however, Aion witnessed futures in which the influence of Heaven gradually diminished.

Not through destruction.

Through change.

Fearful of what might be lost, Aion repeatedly delayed the fulfillment of the Covenant's original purpose.

Again and again he answered the same question:

> "Not yet."

Whether history will remember him as wise or fearful remains unknown.

**Philosophy:**  
Everything arrives in its proper season.

---

## Velora, Weaver of Paths

**Domain:** Fate

**Titles:**

- Weaver of Paths
    
- The Threadmother
    
- Keeper of Destinies
    
- Lady of the Loom
    

**Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

Velora governs fate, possibility, and the countless paths a life may follow.

Many believe every mortal life is represented by a thread within her cosmic loom.

Whether fate is absolute or merely guidance remains one of the great mysteries surrounding her.

She frequently clashes with [[Vaelor|Vaelor, the Open Hand]] regarding the nature of free will.

Their philosophical conflict remains one of the defining disputes within [[The Great Divide]].

Unlike many who speak of destiny, Velora does not believe the future should be controlled.

She believes possibility itself must be preserved.

As the original purpose of the Covenant began fading from memory, Velora quietly scattered fragments of truth throughout history.

Dreams.

Visions.

Prophecies.

Forgotten records.

Not enough to dictate the future.

Only enough to ensure that one remained possible.

**Philosophy:**  
Every choice creates a path, but not every path may be walked.

---

## Tharos, the Golden Law

**Domain:** Authority

**Titles:**

- The Golden Law
    
- Lord of Thrones
    
- Keeper of Order
    
- The Crown Eternal
    

**Alignment:** [[The Concordant]]

Tharos embodies authority, hierarchy, governance, law, and structure.

Kings, emperors, judges, and rulers often invoke his name.

He believes civilization flourishes only when order is maintained.

His followers view law as the foundation of peace and prosperity.

His critics accuse him of valuing stability more than freedom.

His teachings form one of the philosophical cornerstones of [[The Concordant]].

Among the First Twelve, Tharos has become the strongest defender of the Covenant's continuation.

Unlike Aion, Tharos is not burdened by uncertainty.

He understands the original promise.

He understands the sacrifice.

He understands the truth.

Yet he believes preserving civilization outweighs honoring an ancient agreement.

To Tharos, the greatest responsibility of leadership is protecting what has been built.

Even when doing so requires difficult choices.

**Philosophy:**  
Order creates prosperity.

---

## Elyndra, Keeper of Truths

**Domain:** Truth

**Titles:**

- Keeper of Truths
    
- The Unbroken Mirror
    
- Lady of Revelation
    
- The Crystal Voice
    

**Alignment:** [[The Concordant]]

Elyndra embodies truth, knowledge, revelation, and understanding.

She is revered by scholars, historians, judges, investigators, and seekers of wisdom.

Unlike many of her siblings, Elyndra values reality as it truly exists rather than as it is remembered or believed.

Truth, to Elyndra, exists independently of opinion.

It may be hidden.

Ignored.

Buried.

Forgotten.

Yet it remains.

For much of history Elyndra viewed the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]] as one of Heaven's greatest achievements.

Only recently did she begin noticing contradictions within ancient records.

Missing names.

Altered histories.

Events that no longer fully made sense.

Over countless ages these inconsistencies accumulated until Elyndra reached a troubling conclusion:

The historical record itself had been changed.

Today, Elyndra quietly pursues a mystery she cannot fully explain.

She knows something has been forgotten.

She knows someone has been erased.

She simply does not yet know why.

Among the First Twelve, she may be one of the closest to uncovering the truth.

**Philosophy:**  
Truth exists whether it is welcomed or not.

---

## Vaelor, the Open Hand

**Domain:** Choice

**Titles:**

- The Open Hand
    
- Lord of Freedom
    
- The Pathmaker
    
- The Unbound
    

**Alignment:** [[The Ascendant]]

Vaelor embodies free will, independence, self-determination, and possibility.

Travelers, explorers, rebels, and pioneers often revere him.

He teaches that every life gains meaning through the ability to choose.

His disagreements with [[Velora|Velora, Weaver of Paths]] and [[Tharos|Tharos, the Golden Law]] are among the oldest conflicts in existence.

His philosophy forms one of the cornerstones of [[The Ascendant]].

Though he accepted the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]], he never believed it should become permanent.

To Vaelor, a future that cannot be chosen is no future at all.

**Philosophy:**  
No path has meaning unless it can be rejected.

---

## Morwyn, Lady of Dreams

**Domain:** Dreams

**Titles:**

- Lady of Dreams
    
- The Moon-Sleeper
    
- Keeper of Visions
    
- The Silver Veil
    

**Alignment:** [[The Ascendant]]

Morwyn governs dreams, imagination, prophecy, inspiration, and creativity.

Artists, prophets, poets, and visionaries frequently seek her guidance.

Many believe dreams serve as a bridge between reality and possibility.

Of all the First Twelve, Morwyn is perhaps the most difficult to understand.

Even her followers rarely know where dreams end and prophecy begins.

Many of the visions later preserved by [[Velora|Velora, Weaver of Paths]] first emerged through Morwyn's domain.

Some scholars believe that while Aion sees what will be, Morwyn glimpses what could be.

In recent ages, Morwyn has become increasingly troubled by recurring visions.

A forgotten throne.

A sealed door.

A patient figure waiting beyond it.

And a voice that repeatedly asks the same question:

> "Has it been long enough?"

She does not yet understand what the dream means.

She fears she soon will.

**Philosophy:**  
Reality begins in the realm of dreams.

---

## Kael'Thur, Lord of Strife

**Domain:** Conflict

**Titles:**

- Lord of Strife
    
- The Crimson Hand
    
- The Iron Challenger
    
- The Unyielding Flame
    

**Alignment:** [[The Ascendant]]

Kael'Thur embodies conflict, struggle, ambition, competition, and perseverance.

Contrary to popular belief, he is not merely a god of war.

He believes growth requires resistance.

Without hardship, strength cannot exist.

Without challenge, greatness cannot emerge.

Warriors often honor him, but so do athletes, craftsmen, and leaders.

Though many fear conflict, Kael'Thur sees it as a necessary force of transformation.

His disagreements with [[Tharos|Tharos, the Golden Law]] have shaped countless civilizations throughout history.

As the Covenant endures beyond its original purpose, Kael'Thur increasingly questions whether reality can continue growing without facing its next great challenge.

**Philosophy:**  
Strength is forged through adversity.

---

## Thaloris, the Ever-Tide

**Domain:** Change

**Titles:**

- The Ever-Tide
    
- Lady of Transformation
    
- The Endless Current
    
- Keeper of Seasons
    

**Alignment:** [[The Ascendant]]

Thaloris embodies change, adaptation, evolution, and transformation.

Her symbol is often depicted as an ocean wave.

She teaches that nothing survives unchanged.

Those who resist change eventually break beneath it.

Her followers are common among sailors, explorers, innovators, and reformers.

Among the First Twelve, few are more accepting of uncertainty.

To Thaloris, every ending contains the possibility of a beginning.

Every age eventually gives way to another.

While she once supported the Covenant's purpose, she never believed it should remain forever.

To Thaloris, stability exists to prepare creation for transformation.

Not to prevent it.

**Philosophy:**  
Nothing survives unchanged.

---

## Ilyria, Heart of Life

**Domain:** Life

**Titles:**

- Heart of Life
    
- The Green Mother
    
- Lady of Blooming Fields
    
- Keeper of Growth
    

**Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

Ilyria embodies life, healing, fertility, growth, renewal, and compassion.

She is among the most widely worshipped of the First Twelve.

Farmers, healers, families, and communities often seek her blessing.

She shares an ancient bond with [[Nethrys|Nethrys, the Quiet End]].

Their relationship is considered one of the most harmonious among the First Twelve and is documented within [[Divine Relationships]].

Ilyria understands better than most that growth requires balance.

Life cannot exist without death.

Beginnings cannot exist without endings.

Because of this, she often finds herself acting as a mediator during divine disputes.

In recent ages she has increasingly questioned whether the Covenant still allows life to flourish as it once did.

A seed cannot remain buried forever.

A child cannot remain sheltered forever.

At some point, growth requires trust.

**Philosophy:**  
Life seeks to flourish wherever it can.

---

## Nethrys, the Quiet End

**Domain:** Death

**Titles:**

- The Quiet End
    
- Shepherd of Souls
    
- The Final Door
    
- Keeper of Rest
    

**Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

Nethrys embodies death, endings, transition, acceptance, and completion.

Among mortals she is often misunderstood.

Many fear death.

Nethrys does not.

To her, endings are not failures.

They are necessary parts of existence.

Without endings, beginnings lose meaning.

Without completion, growth cannot continue.

Nethrys guides souls from one existence to the next and ensures the natural cycle continues.

Many depictions portray her as calm, patient, and deeply compassionate.

As the Covenant endured beyond its intended purpose, Nethrys became increasingly troubled.

Not because the Covenant failed.

Because it succeeded.

Some things are meant to end.

Not because they are mistakes.

Because they have fulfilled their purpose.

Whether the Covenant has reached that point remains one of the defining questions of the age.

**Philosophy:**  
An ending is not the opposite of a beginning. It is the reason beginnings matter.

## The Missing Thirteenth

Ancient myths occasionally reference a lost figure absent from nearly all surviving records.

Most scholars dismiss these stories as symbolic folklore.

Others believe they reference a forgotten deity.

Fragments of ancient texts speak of a being associated with transformation, possibility, and the passing of ages.

A figure whose name appears only in damaged records, corrupted prophecies, and scattered legends.

The identity of this being has been lost.

Or perhaps hidden.

Most theologians consider such stories impossible.

Yet the persistence of these myths has led some scholars to wonder whether history itself may be incomplete.

Among those willing to ask difficult questions, this mystery remains one of the oldest unanswered questions in existence.

See: [[Xyros, The Final Turning]]

## Influence Upon The Celestial Court

The First Twelve rarely intervene directly in mortal affairs.

Instead, their philosophies and influence shape the actions of [[The Celestial Court]], whose members govern much of Heaven's day-to-day affairs.

The Primordials are not rulers in the traditional sense.

They are foundational forces.

The Celestial Court exists because beings of such immense scope cannot reasonably administer the countless responsibilities of creation directly.

As a result, the Court governs according to principles inspired by the First Twelve.

Over countless ages, these philosophies gave rise to competing interpretations regarding duty, freedom, change, truth, life, and destiny.

Many of these disagreements are reflected within the politics of Heaven itself.

The continuing tensions of [[The Great Divide]] influence not only the First Twelve, but the Court that serves beneath them.

Most mortals worship members of the Court directly while reserving reverence for the First Twelve themselves.

Though distant, the Primordials remain the ultimate foundation of all divine authority.

See: [[The Celestial Court]]

## The Twelve Anchors

When Umbros accepted the burden of the Covenant, portions of his essence were entrusted to the First Twelve.

Each sibling became guardian of a different aspect of who he was.

Together these fragments became known as the Twelve Anchors.

Though originally safeguarded by the Primordials themselves, the Anchors eventually became scattered throughout creation.

Their purpose was never imprisonment.

Their purpose was preservation.

Many scholars know nothing of the Anchors.

Those who do rarely understand their true significance.

See: [[The Anchors of Umbros]]

---

## Legacy

The First Twelve are more than gods.

They are the philosophical foundations of reality itself.

Every civilization reflects their influence.

Every religion interprets their teachings differently.

Every age is shaped by their disagreements.

Though the world often views them as distant and eternal, the First Twelve remain deeply invested in the future of creation.

Yet they do not agree on what that future should become.

Some seek stability.

Some seek transformation.

Others seek wisdom between the two.

As ancient promises resurface and forgotten truths emerge, many believe the long-unresolved tensions among the First Twelve are approaching a turning point.

Whether that turning point will save reality or transform it forever remains unknown.
