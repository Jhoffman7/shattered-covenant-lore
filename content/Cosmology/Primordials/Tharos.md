# Tharos

**Title:** The Golden Law

**Domains:** Authority, Civilization, Order, Law

**Alignment:** [[The Concordant]]

**Symbols:**

- A golden crown above a balanced scale
    
- A throne carved from stone
    
- A closed gate protected by twelve pillars
    
- A golden scepter wrapped in chains
    

---

## Overview

Tharos is the Primordial of Authority, Law, Civilization, and Order.

Among the First Twelve, he is regarded as the foremost architect of stable society.

Kings invoke his name when they ascend to power.

Judges invoke his name before rendering judgment.

Empires often claim his blessing.

Where others see individuals, Tharos sees civilizations.

Where others see moments, Tharos sees generations.

He believes that humanity's greatest achievements are not acts of heroism.

They are institutions.

Laws.

Traditions.

Systems capable of enduring beyond the lives of those who created them.

Many mortals consider him stern.

Others consider him wise.

Few would describe him as compassionate.

Yet those who know him best understand that his devotion to order comes from a genuine desire to protect creation.

---

## Personality

Tharos is disciplined, pragmatic, and unwavering.

He rarely allows emotion to influence his decisions.

He believes leadership requires sacrifice.

Sometimes the sacrifice of comfort.

Sometimes the sacrifice of happiness.

Sometimes the sacrifice of ideals.

Unlike many of his siblings, Tharos accepts that difficult choices are unavoidable.

He does not seek perfection.

He seeks stability.

Among the First Twelve, he is often viewed as one of the most reliable.

He is also among the least likely to change his mind once a decision has been made.

---

## The Purpose Of Civilization

To Tharos, civilization is one of creation's greatest accomplishments.

Cities allow knowledge to survive.

Laws reduce suffering.

Institutions preserve wisdom across generations.

Without order, every generation begins again from nothing.

Without stability, progress becomes temporary.

For this reason, Tharos views governance as a sacred responsibility.

Power exists to protect.

Authority exists to preserve.

Leadership exists to serve something greater than oneself.

---

## Sol'Vara

Among the First Twelve, few relationships are more complicated than Tharos' relationship with [[Sol'Vara]].

Both care deeply for creation.

Both wish to see civilization flourish.

Yet they fundamentally disagree on how best to achieve that goal.

Sol'Vara sees possibility.

Tharos sees responsibility.

Sol'Vara asks what the future could become.

Tharos asks what might be lost.

Neither views the other as malicious.

Yet their disagreement has become one of the defining tensions within [[The Great Divide]].

---

## The Original Covenant

Tharos strongly supported the creation of the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]].

Reality was unstable.

Civilization remained fragile.

History struggled to endure.

The Covenant offered something unprecedented.

Time.

For the first time, civilizations could grow without fear that reality itself might change beneath them.

To Tharos, the Covenant represented one of Heaven's greatest achievements.

Not because it created perfection.

Because it created continuity.

---

## Umbros

Unlike many modern defenders of the Covenant, Tharos remembers the original promise.

He remembers [[Umbros]].

He remembers the sacrifice.

He remembers that the burden was intended to be temporary.

This knowledge does not comfort him.

It burdens him.

Because Tharos genuinely respects Umbros.

Perhaps more than most realize.

He understands the cost.

He understands the injustice.

Yet he remains unwilling to end the Covenant.

Not because he has forgotten.

Because he believes the alternative is worse.

---

## The Age Beyond Heaven

The future that troubles [[Aion]] does not frighten Tharos for the same reasons.

Aion fears what may happen.

Tharos fears what may be lost.

In the futures beyond the Covenant, Tharos sees institutions weakening.

Traditions fading.

Authority fragmenting.

The influence of Heaven diminishing.

He does not believe this future guarantees catastrophe.

He simply believes it carries unacceptable risk.

To Tharos, protecting civilization requires preserving the structures that made civilization possible.

Even if those structures are imperfect.

---

## The Modern Age

Among the First Twelve, Tharos has become the most prominent defender of the Covenant's continuation.

Many within [[The Concordant]] look to him for leadership.

Some quietly question his conclusions.

Few question his conviction.

Tharos understands the arguments of the Ascendant.

He understands the concerns of the Balance.

He simply believes they underestimate the dangers of uncertainty.

If forced to choose between honoring a forgotten promise and preserving civilization, Tharos knows which choice he will make.

The fact that the decision pains him does not change it.

---

## Worship

Tharos is commonly revered by:

- Kings
    
- Judges
    
- Lawmakers
    
- Military Officers
    
- Administrators
    
- Nobility
    
- Civic Leaders
    

His temples often function as courthouses, government halls, and centers of public administration.

His followers believe order is a public good and that stability is necessary for prosperity.

---

## Common Teachings

- Civilization is humanity's greatest achievement.
    
- Leadership is responsibility, not privilege.
    
- Stability creates opportunity.
    
- Laws exist to protect the vulnerable.
    
- A flawed order is often preferable to chaos.
    

---

## Philosophy

> Order creates prosperity.
