# Vaelor

**Title:** The Open Hand

**Domains:** Freedom, Choice, Liberty, Self-Determination

**Alignment:** [[The Ascendant]]

**Symbols:**

- An open hand releasing a bird
    
- A broken chain resting upon a road
    
- A key suspended between two paths
    
- A door standing open beneath the sky
    

---

## Overview

Vaelor is the Primordial of Freedom, Choice, Liberty, and Self-Determination.

He embodies the belief that every being possesses the right to determine its own path.

Among the First Twelve, Vaelor is often regarded as one of the most passionate defenders of individual will.

Kings and peasants.

Saints and criminals.

Heroes and villains.

All possess the freedom to choose.

To Vaelor, that freedom is sacred.

Not because every choice is wise.

But because choice itself gives meaning to existence.

Without the ability to choose, virtue becomes obedience.

Without the ability to fail, success loses value.

Without freedom, life becomes performance rather than purpose.

---

## Personality

Vaelor is charismatic, idealistic, and fiercely independent.

He values authenticity above conformity.

Conviction above obedience.

He rarely tells others what they should do.

Instead, he challenges them to decide for themselves.

Many find him inspiring.

Others find him frustrating.

Vaelor often refuses to provide answers when answers are requested.

He believes wisdom gained through personal discovery is more valuable than wisdom imposed by authority.

Among the First Twelve, he is one of the least comfortable with control.

Even when exercised with good intentions.

---

## The Nature Of Freedom

Vaelor teaches that freedom is not the absence of responsibility.

It is the ability to accept responsibility for one's own choices.

Freedom includes risk.

Failure.

Regret.

Consequences.

Many who criticize Vaelor point to the dangers of unrestricted liberty.

Vaelor does not deny those dangers.

He simply believes that a life without meaningful choice is a greater danger still.

For this reason, he often finds himself opposed to systems that seek to protect people by limiting their agency.

---

## Tharos

No rivalry among the First Twelve is older than that between Vaelor and [[Tharos]].

Both desire prosperous civilizations.

Both wish to reduce suffering.

Both seek to help creation flourish.

Yet they disagree on a fundamental question.

Should order shape freedom?

Or should freedom shape order?

Tharos believes civilization depends upon structure.

Vaelor believes civilization exists to serve individuals.

Neither has ever convinced the other.

Their debate has influenced governments, religions, and political movements throughout history.

---

## Velora

Vaelor shares another longstanding philosophical disagreement with [[Velora]].

Both care deeply about the future.

Both defend the importance of choice.

Yet they approach destiny from different directions.

Velora sees countless possible paths.

Vaelor focuses upon the individual who chooses among them.

To Velora, possibility is sacred.

To Vaelor, the act of choosing is sacred.

Though subtle, the distinction remains important to both.

---

## The Original Covenant

Vaelor reluctantly accepted the creation of the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]].

Reality was young.

Civilization was fragile.

Stability was necessary.

Even Vaelor understood that.

Yet he insisted upon one condition.

The Covenant must remain temporary.

The world could be guided.

It could be protected.

It could be given time to mature.

But eventually, reality would need the freedom to determine its own future.

That promise became one of the reasons Vaelor agreed to support the Covenant at all.

---

## The Forgotten Choice

As centuries became millennia, Vaelor grew increasingly uneasy.

The Covenant remained.

The promise faded.

The future narrowed.

More and more decisions were made in the name of preserving stability.

At first these actions seemed reasonable.

Then necessary.

Then unquestionable.

That transformation troubled him deeply.

Because the greatest threat to freedom is rarely tyranny.

It is the belief that freedom is no longer necessary.

---

## The Modern Age

Among the First Twelve, Vaelor has become one of the strongest advocates for ending the Covenant.

Not because he knows what lies beyond it.

Not because he expects the future to be better.

Because he believes the future belongs to those who must live within it.

Whether the world prospers.

Whether it struggles.

Whether it succeeds or fails.

Those outcomes should emerge from the choices of creation itself.

Not from a decision made thousands of years ago.

To Vaelor, preserving the Covenant indefinitely has become its own form of imprisonment.

---

## The Anchor Of Choice

The fragment of Umbros entrusted to Vaelor became known as the [[The Anchors of Umbros|Anchor of Choice]].

Among all the Anchors, this one may reflect a truth many have forgotten.

Umbros chose.

No one forced him.

No one compelled him.

His sacrifice possessed meaning because it was freely given.

Without choice, sacrifice becomes obligation.

Without freedom, duty loses its moral weight.

Vaelor considers this lesson one of the most important truths contained within the Anchors.

---

## Worship

Vaelor is commonly revered by:

- Travelers
    
- Adventurers
    
- Revolutionaries
    
- Explorers
    
- Philosophers
    
- Pioneers
    
- Free Cities
    

His temples are often modest and decentralized.

Many followers prefer shrines placed along roads, crossroads, and places of decision.

Followers are encouraged to take responsibility for their choices and live authentically.

---

## Common Teachings

- Freedom carries responsibility.
    
- Choice gives life meaning.
    
- No path is without consequence.
    
- Wisdom cannot be forced upon others.
    
- A future worth having must be chosen.
    

---

## Philosophy

> A choice only matters if it can be refused.
