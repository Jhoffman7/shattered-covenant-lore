# Aion

**Title:** Keeper of Ages

**Domains:** Time, Memory, History, Continuity

**Alignment:** [[The Concordant]]

**Symbols:**

- An hourglass encircled by an unbroken ring
    
- A clock without hands
    
- A spiral of twelve stars
    

---

## Overview

Aion is the Primordial of Time and the guardian of memory, history, and continuity.

Among the First Twelve, few possess a broader understanding of reality.

He witnesses the rise and fall of civilizations.

The birth and death of stars.

The unfolding of ages.

To Aion, existence is not a collection of isolated moments.

It is a single, continuous story.

Because of this, he often sees consequences others overlook.

Many cultures depict him as a patient observer seated beside the river of time itself.

Others portray him as a cosmic archivist who remembers every moment that has ever occurred.

Whether these depictions are literal or symbolic remains unknown.

---

## Personality

Aion is thoughtful, deliberate, and exceptionally patient.

He rarely speaks without purpose.

He rarely acts without consideration.

Unlike many gods associated with prophecy, Aion does not seek control.

He seeks understanding.

He values perspective above passion and continuity above impulse.

Among his siblings, he is often viewed as one of the wisest.

Yet even wisdom carries burdens.

The longer Aion exists, the more difficult it becomes for him to distinguish caution from fear.

---

## Before The Covenant

Before the creation of the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]], reality existed in a state of constant transformation.

Civilizations rose and vanished rapidly.

Magic changed unpredictably.

History struggled to endure.

Aion became increasingly concerned that creation lacked sufficient stability to mature.

He believed reality required time.

Not eternity.

Simply time.

From this concern emerged the idea that would eventually become the Covenant.

---

## Architect Of The Covenant

It was Aion who first proposed the Covenant.

His intention was never imprisonment.

His intention was never permanence.

The Covenant was designed as a temporary measure.

A period of stability during which civilization could establish roots.

A period during which history could accumulate rather than endlessly restart.

Many among the First Twelve agreed.

Others remained skeptical.

Ultimately, the Covenant was forged.

---

## Umbros

No relationship has shaped Aion's legacy more than his bond with [[Umbros]].

When Umbros volunteered to become the Covenant's anchor, Aion accepted the sacrifice.

Both believed the burden would eventually end.

Both believed the promise would be honored.

As ages passed, Umbros repeatedly turned to Aion with the same question:

> "Has it been long enough?"

Again and again, Aion answered:

> "Not yet."

Those two words would eventually shape the fate of creation.

---

## The Future He Saw

As the ages progressed, Aion continued observing possible futures.

Many were beautiful.

Many were terrible.

One future appeared more frequently than any other.

A future beyond the Covenant.

A future in which the influence of Heaven gradually diminished.

Not through destruction.

Not through rebellion.

Through irrelevance.

Mortals increasingly governed themselves.

New forms of magic emerged.

Dragon Regents lost portions of their authority.

The world continued moving forward.

But Heaven no longer stood at its center.

---

## The Long Silence

At first, Aion's hesitation was temporary.

A few decades.

Then a few centuries.

Then longer.

Each delay felt reasonable.

Each extension seemed prudent.

Over time, the original purpose of the Covenant faded from memory.

The promise weakened.

The burden remained.

Aion understood what was happening.

Aion said nothing.

---

## Regret

Aion does not view himself as innocent.

Nor does he view himself as a villain.

He understands the consequences of his choices.

He understands what Umbros sacrificed.

He understands what Sol'Vara lost.

He understands what was forgotten.

The burden of this knowledge has become one of the defining aspects of his existence.

Yet even now, Aion remains uncertain.

He still does not know whether he protected the world.

Or merely delayed its future.

---

## The Modern Age

Among the First Twelve, few are more isolated than Aion.

Many still respect his wisdom.

Some quietly question his judgment.

Others fear the answers he possesses.

Within [[The Concordant]], Aion remains one of the most influential voices.

Yet even his allies increasingly sense uncertainty within him.

The confidence that once guided the Covenant has begun to crack.

And beneath those cracks lies a question he has avoided for millennia.

A question first asked by Sol'Vara.

A question repeated by Umbros.

A question that may soon demand an answer.

> Has it been long enough?

---

## Worship

Aion is commonly revered by:

- Historians
    
- Archivists
    
- Scholars
    
- Chroniclers
    
- Judges
    
- Record Keepers
    
- Astronomers
    

His temples often function as libraries, observatories, and repositories of knowledge.

Many followers view preserving history as a sacred duty.

---

## Common Teachings

- Every action becomes part of history.
    
- Wisdom requires perspective.
    
- Memory is the foundation of civilization.
    
- Time reveals what passion obscures.
    
- Some decisions can only be understood in hindsight.
    

---

## Philosophy

> Everything arrives in its proper season.
