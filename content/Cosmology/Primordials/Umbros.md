# Umbros

**Title:** The Hollow King

**Domains:** Void, Sacrifice, Endings, Restraint

**Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

**Symbols:**

- A black circle containing a silver star
    
- A broken crown suspended in darkness
    
- A closed gate beneath a night sky
    

---

## Overview

Umbros is the Primordial of Void, Sacrifice, Restraint, and Consequence.

Though many mortals associate him with darkness, Umbros is not a god of evil.

He embodies limits.

Boundaries.

The truths that exist beyond comfort.

The consequences that follow every choice.

Where Sol'Vara creates possibilities, Umbros asks what those possibilities will cost.

Among the First Twelve, Umbros is often considered one of the wisest.

Not because he knows the most.

Because he is willing to acknowledge truths others would rather ignore.

Many theologians believe creation itself could not exist without him.

Without limits, creation has no shape.

Without endings, beginnings lose meaning.

Without consequence, choice becomes meaningless.

---

## Personality

Umbros is thoughtful, patient, and deeply reflective.

Unlike many gods associated with darkness, he rarely acts through fear or intimidation.

He is calm.

Measured.

Deliberate.

He prefers difficult truths to comforting lies.

Many who meet him describe him as compassionate in an unusual way.

He rarely tells others what they want to hear.

He tells them what they need to hear.

Among the First Twelve, he is often viewed as the sibling most willing to carry burdens others cannot.

---

## Before Creation

Ancient myths place Umbros beside [[Sol'Vara]] before the creation of reality itself.

Many stories describe them as the first two beings.

Light and Shadow.

Creation and Void.

Possibility and Consequence.

Though the details vary across cultures, nearly every creation myth portrays them together.

Their relationship is widely regarded as one of the oldest bonds in existence.

Some traditions teach that reality itself emerged from the tension between them.

Others teach that reality was born from their union.

Regardless of interpretation, few relationships have shaped creation more profoundly.

---

## The Original Covenant

When debate surrounding [[Xyros, The Final Turning]] threatened to divide the First Twelve, Umbros supported the creation of the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]].

He understood the concerns of all sides.

Reality was young.

Civilization was fragile.

The future remained uncertain.

Yet when the Covenant required an anchor, no consensus could be reached.

Someone would have to bear the burden.

Someone would have to become the lock.

Umbros volunteered.

No one demanded it.

No one forced it upon him.

He chose.

He believed the burden was necessary.

More importantly, he believed it would be temporary.

---

## The Promise

Many later histories portray Umbros as a prisoner.

This is inaccurate.

Umbros did not accept imprisonment.

He accepted a promise.

The Covenant was never intended to endure forever.

When its purpose had been fulfilled, the burden would end.

Reality would continue.

The future would move forward.

Umbros trusted this promise.

That trust would shape the fate of creation.

---

## Becoming The Anchor

The burden of the Covenant could not be contained by divine will alone.

To preserve both the Covenant and himself, portions of Umbros' essence were separated and transformed into sacred anchors.

These relics would later become known as [[The Anchors of Umbros]].

His memories.

His hopes.

His identity.

His promises.

His capacity for doubt.

His love.

Each became part of the structure that sustained the Covenant.

At the moment of the signing, Umbros ceased to be merely a Primordial.

His essence became intertwined with reality itself.

---
## The Twelve Anchors

When Umbros accepted the burden of the Covenant, portions of his essence were entrusted to each of the First Twelve.

Each sibling became the guardian of a different aspect of who he was.

Love.

Memory.

Hope.

Truth.

Duty.

Choice.

And many others.

Together these fragments became known as the Twelve Anchors.

Though originally safeguarded by the Primordials themselves, the Anchors eventually became scattered throughout creation.

Their purpose was never imprisonment.

Their purpose was preservation.

Should the Anchors ever be reunited, Umbros would not become stronger.

He would become whole.

---
## Aion

Among the First Twelve, Umbros trusted [[Aion]] more than any other regarding the Covenant.

Aion proposed it.

Aion understood time.

Aion would know when the appointed hour had arrived.

Again and again, throughout the ages, Umbros asked the same question:

> "Has it been long enough?"

Again and again, Aion answered:

> "Not yet."

And because Umbros trusted him, the burden continued.

---

## Fragmentation

As centuries became millennia, the Covenant slowly consumed portions of Umbros' identity.

Memories blurred.

Thoughts fragmented.

Pieces of his essence became inseparable from the structure he maintained.

Many eventually forgot him.

Some remembered him only as a myth.

Others remembered only fragments.

Yet the burden endured.

And so did Umbros.

---

## The Modern Age

Few beings fully understand what remains of Umbros.

Some believe he no longer exists.

Others believe he became one with the Covenant.

The truth lies somewhere between.

Though fragmented and diminished, Umbros continues to endure.

Still waiting.

Still trusting.

Still believing the promise will eventually be honored.

---

## Worship

Umbros is among the least openly worshipped of the First Twelve.

His followers are often:

- Philosophers
    
- Judges
    
- Monastics
    
- Hermits
    
- Scholars
    
- Guardians
    
- Oathkeepers
    

His temples tend to be quiet places dedicated to contemplation, self-discipline, and truth.

Many followers view sacrifice as the highest expression of responsibility.

---

## Common Teachings

- Every choice carries a consequence.
    
- Boundaries give meaning to freedom.
    
- Sacrifice is the price of responsibility.
    
- Some burdens are worth carrying.
    
- Truth remains truth even when ignored.
    

---
