# Throne of Memory

**Function:** Preservation of History, Knowledge, Records, and Collective Memory

**Associated Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

**Common Titles:**

- Keepers of Memory
    
- The Eternal Archive
    
- Guardians of the First Record
    
- Wardens of History
    
- The Unbroken Ledger
    

---

## Overview

The Throne of Memory serves as the custodian of Heaven's historical record.

Its responsibility is simple in theory:

Preserve the truth of what happened.

In practice, the office has become one of the most powerful and influential institutions within the [[The Celestial Court]].

Every law requires precedent.

Every prophecy requires historical context.

Every treaty requires documentation.

Every investigation requires evidence.

As a result, nearly every Throne depends upon the archives maintained by the Throne of Memory.

Though often perceived as scholars and archivists, the Keepers of Memory wield enormous influence through the information they preserve.

Knowledge shapes decisions.

Memory shapes knowledge.

In many ways, the Throne of Memory quietly shapes Heaven itself.

---

## Core Responsibilities

The Throne of Memory is responsible for:

- Preserving historical records
    
- Maintaining celestial archives
    
- Recording major events
    
- Safeguarding ancient treaties
    
- Cataloging divine artifacts
    
- Maintaining genealogies and lineages
    
- Preserving records of saints and heroes
    
- Protecting foundational documents of Heaven
    
- Ensuring historical continuity across ages
    

No record is considered too small.

No event is considered entirely insignificant.

The Throne's purpose is preservation.

Not interpretation.

Or so its members claim.

---

## Authority

Unlike [[Throne of Vigil]], the Throne of Memory commands no armies.

Unlike [[Throne of Governance]], it writes no laws.

Unlike [[Throne of Judgment]], it passes no rulings.

Yet few Thrones possess greater influence.

The Throne of Memory controls access to the historical foundation upon which nearly all major decisions are made.

Without records:

Law loses precedent.

Prophecy loses context.

Doctrine loses authority.

Investigation loses evidence.

Because of this, many observers consider the Throne of Memory one of the most politically powerful institutions in Heaven.

Its influence is rarely obvious.

Yet it is nearly impossible to avoid.

---

## Relationship To The First Twelve

The Throne of Memory has historically maintained strong philosophical ties to [[Elyndra]] and [[Aion]].

Truth requires memory.

Memory gives meaning to truth.

For much of Heaven's history, this relationship appeared harmonious.

In recent ages, however, that relationship has become increasingly complicated.

As contradictions emerge within the archives, questions once dismissed as impossible have begun attracting serious attention.

The Throne now finds itself caught between competing interpretations of history.

---

## The Eternal Archive

At the center of the Throne's authority stands the Eternal Archive.

A vast repository containing records that span the entirety of known existence.

Within its halls are preserved:

- Ancient treaties
    
- Celestial decrees
    
- Records of kingdoms
    
- Histories of saints
    
- Prophecies
    
- Genealogies
    
- Divine correspondence
    

Many scholars consider it the greatest collection of knowledge in creation.

Some claim it contains every significant event ever recorded.

Others believe even the Archive possesses gaps.

Increasingly, those fears appear justified.

---

## The First Record

Among the countless relics preserved within the Eternal Archive exists a singular document known as the First Record.

The document predates nearly every known archive classification system.

Its true purpose remains unknown.

Throughout history, countless archivists, scholars, prophets, judges, and celestial officials have studied it.

No two descriptions of the document perfectly agree.

Some describe it as a treaty.

Others describe it as a prophecy.

Some believe it to be a philosophical text.

Others claim it resembles a divine charter.

Despite centuries of study, no definitive interpretation has ever emerged.

The document appears to reveal different meanings to different readers.

No explanation has ever been universally accepted.

Because of its age and significance, the First Record remains among the most heavily protected artifacts in Heaven.

Many archivists consider it sacred.

Few claim to understand it.

---

## The Contradictions

For much of Heaven's history, inconsistencies within the archives were dismissed as errors.

Copying mistakes.

Translation issues.

Missing records.

Normal imperfections accumulated over immense spans of time.

Yet over the last several ages, the contradictions have become increasingly difficult to ignore.

Missing names.

Events lacking causes.

Treaties referencing unknown parties.

Historical figures appearing and disappearing from records.

Entire chains of evidence terminating without explanation.

No single contradiction proves anything.

Taken together, however, they suggest something deeply unsettling.

History itself may have been altered.

This possibility remains among the most closely guarded concerns within the Throne.

---

## Internal Factions

Though outwardly unified, the Throne of Memory contains several philosophical schools.

### The Preservationists

The oldest and most influential faction.

The Preservationists believe the archives remain fundamentally trustworthy.

Contradictions should be corrected.

Records should be preserved.

History should remain stable.

Most senior archivists belong to this group.

### The Revisionists

The Revisionists believe the contradictions reveal deeper problems.

They argue that portions of Heaven's historical record may be incomplete, damaged, or deliberately altered.

Many seek closer cooperation with followers of [[Elyndra]].

The Preservationists often view them as unnecessarily disruptive.

### The Witnesses

A small and secretive group operating primarily within the deepest levels of the Eternal Archive.

The Witnesses dedicate themselves to cataloging contradictions that cannot be explained through ordinary means.

They possess no unified theory.

No grand conspiracy.

Only questions.

Many of their investigations eventually lead back to the First Record.

This fact troubles them greatly.

---

## Relationship With Elyndra

The relationship between the Throne of Memory and [[Elyndra]] has become increasingly strained.

Historically, the two shared a common purpose.

Preserve truth.

Protect knowledge.

Understand reality.

As Elyndra's investigations intensified, however, she began identifying inconsistencies the Throne struggled to explain.

Many archivists initially dismissed her concerns.

Over time, that became increasingly difficult.

Today, much of the Throne finds itself caught between skepticism and growing unease.

Few openly admit it.

Many secretly fear Elyndra may be correct.

---

## Role In The Covenant Crisis

Officially, the Throne of Memory remains neutral regarding the Covenant.

Its purpose is preservation.

Not politics.

Unofficially, the contradictions emerging throughout the archives have placed the institution at the center of one of Heaven's greatest mysteries.

Many of the missing records.

Altered histories.

Forgotten references.

And unexplained inconsistencies appear connected in ways no one fully understands.

Whether these discoveries represent coincidence, corruption, or something far more significant remains unknown.

The answers may reside within records already under the Throne's protection.

---

## Common Criticisms

The Throne of Memory is frequently accused of:

- Hoarding information
    
- Excessive secrecy
    
- Political manipulation through selective disclosure
    
- Prioritizing preservation over action
    
- Protecting institutions rather than truth
    

Its defenders argue these criticisms misunderstand the burden of safeguarding history.

Its critics argue that history belongs to everyone.
