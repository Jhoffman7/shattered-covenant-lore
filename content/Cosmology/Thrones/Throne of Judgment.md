# Throne of Judgment

**Function:** Justice, Arbitration, Accountability, Legal Interpretation, and Moral Responsibility

**Associated Alignment:** [[The Balance]]

**Common Titles:**

- The Eternal Scales
    
- Keepers of Responsibility
    
- The Hall of Consequence
    
- Wardens of Conviction
    
- The Measured Hand
    

---

## Overview

The Throne of Judgment serves as the primary judicial institution of the [[The Celestial Court]].

Its responsibility extends far beyond punishment.

The Throne exists to determine responsibility.

It interprets celestial law, resolves disputes, evaluates conduct, and examines the consequences of actions taken throughout Heaven.

When questions of legality arise, the Throne provides answers.

When questions of morality arise, the Throne provides perspective.

When questions of responsibility arise, the Throne provides accountability.

Among all institutions within the Court, few command greater respect.

Few inspire greater apprehension.

---

## Purpose

The Celestial Court governs an immense and complex civilization.

Disagreements are inevitable.

Conflicts emerge.

Interpretations differ.

Responsibilities overlap.

Without a mechanism for resolving such disputes, Heaven would quickly descend into dysfunction.

The Throne of Judgment exists to prevent this.

Its role is not merely enforcing laws.

Its role is ensuring those laws continue serving the purpose for which they were created.

---

## Core Responsibilities

The Throne oversees:

- Legal interpretation
    
- Celestial arbitration
    
- Judicial review
    
- Jurisdictional disputes
    
- Ethical evaluations
    
- Accountability proceedings
    
- Review of Regent conduct
    
- Interpretation of ancient charters
    
- Resolution of inter-Throne conflicts
    
- Enforcement of judicial rulings
    

Many of Heaven's most significant disputes ultimately arrive before Judgment.

---

## Authority

Unlike many Thrones, Judgment possesses substantial formal authority.

Its rulings carry legal weight throughout the Celestial Court.

Though the Throne cannot create laws, it possesses broad authority to interpret them.

Its judgments frequently shape how those laws are understood and applied.

As a result, the Throne's influence extends far beyond the courtroom.

Many institutions adjust their behavior according to how they believe Judgment will interpret their actions.

---

## Relationship With Tharos

The Throne maintains deep philosophical ties to [[Tharos]].

Among the First Twelve, Tharos is often associated with duty, conviction, accountability, and consequence.

These principles form the foundation of the Throne's identity.

Members of Judgment frequently teach that justice and responsibility cannot be separated.

A person may be innocent.

A person may be guilty.

But responsibility often exists even where fault does not.

This distinction lies at the heart of many of the Throne's teachings.

---

## The Hall of Consequence

At the center of the Throne stands the Hall of Consequence.

Here, disputes are heard.

Rulings are issued.

Appeals are reviewed.

The Hall serves as both courthouse and philosophical forum.

Many of Heaven's most influential judicial precedents originated within its chambers.

Its records are preserved jointly with the [[Throne of Memory]].

---

## Relationship With The Doctrine of Jurisdiction

The Throne of Judgment serves as the primary interpreter of [[The Doctrine of Jurisdiction]].

While [[Throne of Governance]] administers the doctrine, Judgment determines how it should be applied.

Questions involving:

- Jurisdictional Breaches
    
- Improper information sharing
    
- Authority disputes
    
- Inter-Throne conflicts
    

frequently fall under its review.

Many of the doctrine's most important precedents originated from rulings issued by Judgment.

---

## Relationship With The Throne of Revelation

The relationship between Judgment and [[Throne of Revelation]] is often complicated.

Both seek truth.

Yet their methods differ significantly.

Revelation asks:

> What happened?

Judgment asks:

> Can it be proven?

Investigators frequently become frustrated by the Throne's insistence upon evidence and procedure.

Judges frequently become frustrated by investigators who reach conclusions before securing proof.

Despite these tensions, the two institutions depend heavily upon one another.

---

## Relationship With The Throne of Memory

The Throne of Judgment relies extensively upon the records maintained by [[Throne of Memory]].

Historical evidence often forms the foundation of judicial review.

This dependence has become increasingly troubling in recent ages.

As contradictions within the archives continue to emerge, difficult questions have arisen.

If history itself contains uncertainties, how should responsibility be determined?

Few questions concern Judgment more.

---

## Internal Philosophies

Though united in purpose, the Throne contains several influential schools of thought.

### The Scales

The Scales believe justice requires consistency.

The law must be applied equally.

Exceptions should remain rare.

Personal preference should never outweigh established principle.

Their unofficial motto is:

> Equal law preserves equal justice.

---

### The Lanterns

The Lanterns emphasize intent, context, and mercy.

They argue that understanding why an action occurred is often as important as understanding what occurred.

Their unofficial motto is:

> Wisdom requires understanding.

---

### The Pillars

The Pillars focus upon outcomes and societal stability.

They believe the purpose of law is preserving civilization.

When necessary, they are willing to prioritize practical consequences over strict interpretation.

Their unofficial motto is:

> Justice must endure to matter.

---

## The Covenant Crisis

The Throne of Judgment has become increasingly concerned by developments surrounding the Covenant Crisis.

Questions once considered hypothetical have begun taking tangible form.

Historical contradictions continue to emerge.

Prophetic certainty continues to decline.

Investigative findings continue to challenge accepted assumptions.

For the first time in ages, the Throne finds itself confronting questions that may exceed the boundaries of existing law.

Some within the institution fear an unprecedented possibility.

The Court itself may one day stand in judgment.

---

## The Problem of Responsibility

Among all Thrones, Judgment may be uniquely vulnerable to the truths surrounding the Covenant.

Many institutions ask:

> What happened?

Judgment asks:

> Who bears responsibility?

If the mysteries surrounding the Covenant are eventually revealed, the Throne may be forced to evaluate actions taken by:

- The Celestial Court
    
- The Twelve Thrones
    
- The Dragon Regents
    
- The First Twelve
    
- Perhaps even Heaven itself
    

No precedent exists for such questions.

---

## Reputation

Throughout Heaven, the Throne of Judgment possesses a reputation for seriousness, restraint, and unwavering scrutiny.

Many respect its fairness.

Many fear its conclusions.

A common saying throughout the Court states:

> Truth may explain your actions. Judgment explains their consequences.

---

## Philosophy

> Every choice creates a burden. Every burden deserves an answer.
