# Throne of Stewardship

**Function:** Preservation, Caretaking, Sustainability, Guardianship, and Responsible Custodianship

**Associated Alignment:** [[The Ascendant]]

**Associated Primordial:** [[Ilyria]]

**Common Titles:**

- The Keeper's Throne
    
- Wardens of the Living World
    
- Custodians of Creation
    
- The Gentle Hand
    
- Guardians of Continuity
    

---

## Overview

The Throne of Stewardship serves as the Celestial Court's steward of preservation, sustainability, and responsible care.

Its purpose is not merely to protect creation.

Its purpose is to care for it.

Among the Twelve Thrones, Stewardship concerns itself with everything that has been entrusted to future generations.

Natural environments.

Sacred places.

Communities.

Traditions.

Knowledge.

Institutions.

The Throne teaches that creation is not ownership.

It is responsibility.

What is inherited must be cared for.

What is cared for may endure.

---

## Purpose

The Throne exists to ensure that valuable things survive.

Throughout history, countless wonders have been lost not through malice, but through neglect.

The Throne therefore seeks to cultivate long-term responsibility and sustainable care.

Its responsibilities include:

- Environmental preservation
    
- Resource management
    
- Protection of sacred locations
    
- Cultural continuity
    
- Sustainable development
    
- Long-term planning
    
- Conservation efforts
    
- Restoration projects
    
- Preservation of heritage
    

The Throne focuses not upon immediate gain.

It focuses upon lasting stewardship.

---

## Relationship With Ilyria

The Throne maintains deep philosophical ties to [[Ilyria]].

Among the First Twelve, Ilyria embodies nurture, care, growth, patience, and responsibility.

Members of Stewardship frequently teach that strength is measured not only by what one can build, but by what one can maintain.

The Throne views care itself as a sacred act.

A garden does not flourish because it is planted.

It flourishes because someone continues tending it.

---

## Authority

Stewardship possesses little direct authority.

It rarely commands.

Rarely governs.

Rarely judges.

Its influence comes from expertise, trust, and long-term perspective.

When institutions seek guidance regarding preservation, sustainability, or responsible management, Stewardship is often consulted.

Its members frequently advise other Thrones regarding decisions whose consequences may not become visible for centuries.

---

## The Philosophy of Custodianship

At the heart of Stewardship lies a simple principle:

> We do not inherit creation from those who came before us.
> 
> We borrow it from those who come after us.

This philosophy shapes every aspect of the Throne's work.

Members are taught to think in generations rather than years.

Their focus remains fixed upon the future.

---

## Relationship With Civilization

The Throne of Stewardship works closely with [[Throne of Civilization]].

Civilization builds.

Stewardship maintains.

Civilization creates opportunities.

Stewardship ensures those opportunities endure.

Many of the greatest achievements in history resulted from cooperation between these two institutions.

A common saying shared between them states:

> What is worth building is worth preserving.

---

## Relationship With Vigil

Stewardship and [[Throne of Vigil]] share a mutual respect.

Both seek to protect.

Yet their methods differ.

Vigil protects against threats.

Stewardship protects against neglect.

One responds to danger.

The other prevents decay.

Together they preserve much of what creation values most.

---

## Relationship With Memory

The Throne frequently collaborates with [[Throne of Memory]].

Memory preserves knowledge.

Stewardship preserves what that knowledge represents.

Many historical sites, cultural traditions, and sacred locations survive because of efforts jointly supported by both institutions.

---

## Internal Philosophies

Though united by purpose, Stewardship contains several influential traditions.

### The Keepers

The Keepers focus upon preservation.

They safeguard places, traditions, institutions, and resources deemed vital to future generations.

Their unofficial motto is:

> Preserve what matters.

---

### The Gardeners

The Gardeners emphasize cultivation and growth.

They believe stewardship is not merely protection.

It is helping things flourish.

Their unofficial motto is:

> Care is an act of creation.

---

### The Wardens

The Wardens focus upon guardianship.

They protect locations, resources, and communities placed under their care.

Their unofficial motto is:

> What is entrusted must be defended.

---

## Relationship With Saints

Saints of Stewardship often serve as caretakers, conservationists, community leaders, protectors of sacred places, and guardians of long-term projects.

Their work rarely attracts recognition.

Yet many civilizations owe their survival to generations of faithful stewardship.

The Throne teaches that lasting impact often occurs quietly.

---

## The Covenant Crisis

The Throne views the Covenant Crisis through a uniquely practical lens.

While others seek explanations, Stewardship focuses upon preservation.

If institutions begin faltering, how can they be protected?

If truths are rediscovered, how should they be preserved?

If creation itself begins changing, what responsibilities follow?

Many members quietly believe the coming era will demand unprecedented acts of stewardship.

Not because creation is ending.

Because it may need healing.

---

## The Burden of Care

The Throne teaches that caring for something often requires more effort than creating it.

Creation may occur in a moment.

Preservation may require centuries.

For this reason, Stewardship places extraordinary value upon patience, consistency, and humility.

Many members consider these among the most difficult virtues to practice.

---

## Reputation

Throughout Heaven and the Mortal Realm, the Throne of Stewardship is respected for its patience, reliability, and long-term perspective.

Its members are often viewed as caretakers of things others take for granted.

The Throne accepts this role willingly.

After all, the greatest acts of stewardship are often invisible.

---

## Philosophy

> The truest measure of care is what remains after you are gone.
