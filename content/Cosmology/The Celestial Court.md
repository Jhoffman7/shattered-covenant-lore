# The Celestial Court

The Celestial Court is the governing body of Heaven.

Though many mortals mistakenly believe the First Twelve directly oversee all divine affairs, the reality is far more complex.

The Primordials embody the fundamental principles of existence.

They are not administrators.

They are not bureaucrats.

They are not rulers in the traditional sense.

To govern the countless divine realms, celestial hosts, saints, and faithful souls that exist beneath them, the First Twelve established the Celestial Court.

For millennia, the Court has served as the primary institution through which Heaven interacts with creation.

---

## Purpose

The Court exists to maintain the order of Heaven and oversee the relationship between the divine and mortal worlds.

Its responsibilities include:

- Administration of divine law
    
- Oversight of celestial realms
    
- Recognition of saints
    
- Management of heavenly hosts
    
- Interpretation of divine doctrine
    
- Arbitration of disputes among lesser deities
    
- Stewardship of the Covenant
    

Though its authority originates from the First Twelve, the Court possesses substantial independence in its day-to-day governance.

This arrangement allows Heaven to function even when the Primordials remain distant from mortal affairs.

---

## The Twelve Thrones

The Celestial Court is composed of Twelve Thrones.

Each Throne represents a critical function within the governance of Heaven.

Rather than serving as individual rulers, the Throneholders operate collectively as the primary governing body of the Celestial Court.

Each Throne possesses unique responsibilities, traditions, and authority.

Though inspired by the philosophies of the First Twelve, the Thrones are not direct representatives of any single Primordial.

They serve Heaven as a whole.

Over time, each Throne developed its own culture, priorities, and political interests.

The relationships between the Thrones have become increasingly complex as tensions surrounding the Covenant continue to grow.

## The Covenant

For most of recorded history, the Court has been responsible for maintaining the Covenant established by the First Twelve.

Over time, many members of the Court came to view the Covenant as an essential component of reality itself.

Few remembered its original purpose.

Fewer still remembered the promise upon which it was founded.

As generations of Throneholders succeeded one another, the Covenant gradually transformed from a temporary solution into an unquestioned institution.

By the modern age, many within the Court believe it has always existed.

---

## The Modern Crisis

The Celestial Court stands at the center of a growing crisis.

Ancient records contain contradictions.

Forgotten prophecies have begun resurfacing.

Long-buried truths are emerging.

Whispers spread throughout Heaven concerning a missing god.

A broken promise.

And a future that was never meant to remain sealed forever.

Few understand the full truth.

Many suspect something is wrong.

No one agrees on what should be done.

---

## The Great Divide Within Heaven

The philosophies of [[The Concordant]], [[The Ascendant]], and [[The Balance]] are reflected throughout the Court.

Though the Thrones officially serve Heaven as a whole, individual Throneholders often possess personal loyalties and beliefs.

Some support preserving the Covenant.

Some believe it has fulfilled its purpose.

Others struggle between competing duties.

As tensions continue to rise, the Court grows increasingly fractured.

Many fear that the next great conflict may not begin in the mortal world.

It may begin within Heaven itself.

---

## Relationship To The First Twelve

The Court derives its authority from the First Twelve.

However, the relationship between the Primordials and the Court is intentionally distant.

The Primordials rarely issue direct commands.

The Court is expected to govern according to divine principles rather than constant divine intervention.

This arrangement has endured for millennia.

Whether it remains sufficient for the challenges ahead remains uncertain.
