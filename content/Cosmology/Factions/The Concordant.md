# The Concordant

The Concordant is one of the three great philosophical alignments among the First Twelve.

Its members believe that civilization flourishes through stability, continuity, and order.

The Concordant teaches that creation requires foundations.

Without structure, knowledge cannot accumulate.

Without continuity, history cannot endure.

Without stability, prosperity cannot survive.

Among the First Twelve, the Concordant has historically favored institutions, laws, traditions, and systems capable of enduring across generations.

---

## Core Belief

The Concordant believes that stability is the foundation upon which all other achievements are built.

Growth is valuable.

Freedom is valuable.

Change is valuable.

None of these can endure without order.

The Concordant does not oppose progress.

It opposes uncontrolled progress.

It does not oppose change.

It opposes change for its own sake.

---

## View Of The Covenant

The Concordant supported the creation of the [[The Original Covenant|Original Covenant]].

Most members believed reality required a period of stability in order to mature.

Over time, many members became convinced that the stability created by the Covenant was too valuable to lose.

Though opinions differ among individual members, the Concordant generally views the continuation of the Covenant as necessary for the preservation of civilization.

---

## Modern Position

In the current age, the Concordant represents the strongest defenders of the existing order.

Many fear that ending the Covenant would introduce uncertainty on a scale unseen for thousands of years.

Some members know the original promise.

Others do not.

Most believe that preserving the world is more important than honoring an ancient agreement.

The Concordant does not seek power for its own sake.

It seeks permanence.

---

## Strengths

- Stability
    
- Continuity
    
- Preservation of knowledge
    
- Long-term planning
    
- Civilization building
    

---

## Weaknesses

- Fear of uncertainty
    
- Resistance to necessary change
    
- Attachment to existing institutions
    
- Difficulty relinquishing authority
